      04 / Vulnerabilities

      Vulnerability intelligence

      High and critical CVEs from NVD (CVSS 7+), GitHub Security Advisories, and every recent entry in CISA’s Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, merged by CVE and scored with EPSS.

      How prioritization works
      1. Act now: known exploited. The CVE is in CISA KEV, CISA’s SSVC says exploitation is active, or the CNA reports attacks (CVSS 4.0 E:A). FIRST advises treating these as priority whatever their EPSS.
      2. Likely exploited: EPSS ≥ 10%, or a public exploit or proof of concept is referenced (an NVD “Exploit” reference, CVSS 4.0 E:P, or SSVC “poc”).
      3. Severe: CVSS ≥ 9 with no exploitation signal yet.
      4. Monitor: everything else.

      Within a tier, watched products come first, then higher EPSS, then higher CVSS, then newest. Every row lists the reasons behind its tier. EPSS estimates the probability of exploitation activity against a CVE in the next 30 days; it says nothing about whether your systems are exposed. CVSS measures severity, not risk. The same 10% EPSS line is used by the “Likely exploited” filter, the priority tiers and the matrix.

      Edge (WAF) labels are evidence levels. Potential is a preliminary match: a request-shape weakness (SQL injection, path traversal, SSRF…) on a network attack vector. Documented means a WAF vendor’s rule or virtual-patch page is referenced. Not applicable means the vector or description says it isn’t reached over a web edge. Whether a rule works in your environment is yours to record in each CVE’s panel.

      07 / IOCs

      Indicators of compromise

Requires ABUSECH_AUTH_KEY for the abuse.ch feeds only. Treat as recently reported, not confirmed active in your estate — verify before blocking.
